## What to build

### The heading

1. **`GroupHead` draws the whole group.** It takes the group's body as its children and draws the group's `section` element, putting on it any other attribute it is given (`data-*`, `aria-label`, `ondragover`, `ondrop`), so a view draws no element of its own around a heading.
   - The heading is a strip that stays at the top of the pane's scrolling body while its group scrolls under it (`position: sticky; top: 0`, above the cards). Because it is inside its group's section, the next group's heading takes its place at the end of the group.
   - The strip has rounded corners (`var(--input-radius)`) and pads its words the same in every mode.
   - Without a wallpaper the strip is the pane's background (`--panel`), so cards scrolling under it do not show through.
   - Over a wallpaper it is the one translucent layer `Surface` paints (`--panel-on-wall` with the blur), drawn by `GroupHead` itself. Inside an element that already carries `data-surface` (a tinted pane, a free float) it paints that element's own fill, so what scrolls under it stays hidden and there is still one layer over the picture. The head comment says which fill applies where.
   - Under the strip is a margin of 8 px. It is the only space between a heading and what it heads.
   - The line inside is as now: the fold control, the optional glyph, the label in the group's hue or the muted colour, the count. An optional `actions` snippet draws after the count, at the line's right end, for what acts on the whole group (the Schedules' Add, the Catalogue group's key chord).
   - A label longer than the pane is cut with an ellipsis on one line, as now.
   - A folded group's body is in the page with `hidden="until-found"`, and its `beforematch` event unfolds the group, as Lanes and Band do now; `GroupHead` writes this once for every group.
   - The head comment says all of this and names every view that draws it.
2. **One fold state.** A pane keeps the keys of its folded groups as `folded: string[]` in its pane state, the key Venues and the boards already use. Two helpers in `src/lib/state/workbench/layout/instance.ts` replace the toggles written in `Venues.svelte` and `Projects.svelte`: one reads `folded` from a pane state (a value that is not a list of strings reads as none; a duplicated key reads once), one gives the list with a key added or taken out. Every view below uses them.
   - Dismissed, Expired, the run log, Triaged and Muted keep the flags they have (`dismissedOpen`, `expiredOpen`, `runlogOpen`, `triagedOpen`, `showMuted`), which start closed; a group with no saved fold starts open.
   - A state saved before this step, with no `folded`, reads as nothing folded. No code reads an older shape.
   - A `folded` key no group has any more (a bucket removed from `radar_buckets`, a deck group renamed) is kept in the list and changes nothing.
   - A group whose key is empty (Venues' Other, a bucket row with `key: ''`) folds under the empty key.
3. **A reveal opens its group.** When a view is asked to reveal a card (`reveal` in its pane state, or a press in the table of contents) and the card's group is folded, the view takes that group out of `folded` before it scrolls, so the card is mounted. `revealState` keeps opening Expired and Dismissed; its comment says that a view opens a folded group itself. A search never changes a fold: a folded group stays folded and its heading's count is the count the search leaves. The Audit's card cursor steps over the rows drawn, so it skips a folded section; the report says how by reading `cardCursor`'s caller.
4. **No view frames it.** Every view in item 6 draws `GroupHead` with no element of its own around it and sets no space between a heading and its body: the `heading` div of `Projects.svelte` and its styles; the `Surface` around the heading in `Lanes.svelte`, `Band.svelte`, `Venues.svelte`, `Inbox.svelte`, `Linkedin.svelte`, `Pillars.svelte`, `Gallery.svelte` and the record and project pages; the margins named in "What is on the tree" (Lanes, Band, Venues, Bucket, Radar, Leads, Audit's `.group` gap, Pillars' `section` margin and its `h3` style).
