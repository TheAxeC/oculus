## What to build

1. **No frame size anywhere.** The setting `defaults.frame` is gone from `config/defaults/present.yml` and `config/options/present.yml` (its two entries and the `defaults.frame` help line), so the Settings view no longer draws the section "Slides" with its Width and Height controls; the head comment and the `defaults` help no longer name the frame. `src/lib/data/sources/config/settings/present.ts` is removed with its export, its callers (`decks.ts`, `problems.ts`) and its tests. The type `DeckFrame` and every `frame` field of a deck are gone: the server's deck (`model-deck.ts`, `index.ts`), the page's schema (`present.ts:602-605`), `spec.ts`, `read.ts` with `frameOf`, the four finding texts `findings.yml:137-140`, and `desktop.ts`. The field `frame` leaves `config/schemas/deck-schema.yml`; the deck reader does not read the key, so a deck file that still holds it is marked by the editor's schema and ignored by the Present view, and no code reads, converts or tolerates it (the rules file's "No migration code").
2. **One shape.** The slide's shape is 16:9, written once as a constant (one module the page and the desktop host both import, or one for each where the layers forbid a shared import, each naming the other in its comment), and every place that draws, sizes or measures a slide reads it: the Stage, the Overview tiles, the presenter screen (its current slide and its next-slide room), the audience window, the print route and the desktop host's print and audience windows. No other `16 / 9`, `9 / 16`, `1280`, `720`, `960pt` or `540pt` literal stands in the Present code, the print route or the desktop host for the shape or the page.
3. **Sizes as fractions of the slide.** The Stage draws the frame at the largest 16:9 box its room holds, at that box's own size, with no `transform: scale`. The element that holds the frame at that size is a CSS size container (`container-type: size`), and every size inside a slide is in container units of it (`cqw`): a value of N px in the 1280-wide frame becomes N/1280 of the frame's width (cases under "Decisions"). This covers:
   - `slide.css`: the five design sizes and their rice forms, the reading width, the padding, the slide's chip and table steps `--slide-ui-xs/sm/md` (11, 12, 13 of 1280, and under rice 10, 11, 12 of 1280), and `--radius` and `--radius-sm` set on the frame as fractions (10 and 6 of 1280; rice 3 and 2 of 1280), so the frame's corner and every card on a slide follow the slide; the head comment states the fractions and no px box.
   - Every px of the slide parts and of `Panel.svelte`.
   - The chip, the tag and the table where they are drawn on a slide: inside a slide their literal px (`Chip.svelte:146, :151`, `Tag.svelte:87, :90`, `Table.svelte:161, :175, :179`) take the slide's fractions through a rule scoped to the slide frame, and outside a slide they are drawn exactly as today.
   - The boxes the layout places (`Panel.svelte:60-69`, `Plain.svelte:50-61`, `Project.svelte:176-182`) and `Slide.svelte`'s room height, written in container units from the layout's fractions, so a room resized after the layout (a split dragged, a window resized, the audience window going full screen) draws the same boxes with no stale px and no new layout run; `Slide.svelte:285`'s trigger loses `deck.frame` and keeps the slide and its strip.
   - The print route: each `.page` is the size container, so the frame inside it takes the same fractions, its padding included, and `PAGE_PADDING` is gone (the room under the padding is read from the drawn page).
   - The app's text size (`--text-scale`) and the content's factor (`--slide-fit`, 0.75 to 1.25) multiply the sizes as today. Hairline borders of 1px stay 1px (decision 2).
4. **The layout arithmetic in fractions.** `slide-layout.ts` takes the room and every measured length as fractions of the frame's width (Slide.svelte and `drawn-slide.ts` divide each measured px by the frame's drawn width once, at the hand-over), and its constants are fractions: `TEXT_LEAST` 360/1280, `TEXT_GAP` 26/1280, with `Project.svelte`'s use of `TEXT_GAP` in container units. Its head comment's "in the frame's own px" goes; it says the layout is in fractions of the frame's width.
5. **The measure in fractions.** The Stage's measure reports how far the content runs past the frame as a fraction of the frame's height, with no division by a scale. The Present store holds that fraction; `TOO_TALL_PX` becomes a fraction of the frame's height, 1/720 (today's 1 px of a 720 px frame), and `SLACK` 0.5/720. One slide gives one verdict on every surface: the tile's mark, the pane's finding and the presenter screen agree, so a verdict that differs by the size a slide is drawn at is a defect of this step, named in the report with its slide.
6. **The "Slide too tall" finding names no pixel size.** `findings.yml:142` becomes the dictated line under "Dictated text", `{1}` the overrun in percent of the frame's height, rounded to a whole number, at least 1; `present.svelte.ts` passes no frame size.
7. **The PDF.** The print route's `@page` is `size: 960pt 540pt; margin: 0`, written from the shape and one page width constant, the page a 1280 by 720 frame prints at today; a too-tall slide is scaled down to its page as today. `printPdf` takes the address alone: `desktop.ts`, the bridge, `preload.cjs`, `app.ts`, `print.ts` and `export.ts`/`acts.svelte.ts` hand no frame. The hidden print window opens at the page's size in CSS px, derived from the page constant (960 pt is 1280 CSS px at 96 px to the inch, 72 pt to the inch), never written as a separate number. `wholeFrame` and the bridge's frame type go.
8. **The audience window.** `audienceFeatures` takes no frame: the features carry a 16:9 size derived from the screen the page is on (`popup,width=<w>,height=<h>`, `w` half the screen's available width rounded, `h` the shape's height for `w`), so a browser opens a 16:9 window; the desktop host sizes a window with no display of its own to fill from those features as today (`place.ts`), with its own `AUDIENCE_SIZE` height taken from the shape. `frameOf`, `savedFrame` and `createPresenting(frameOf)` lose the frame.
9. **The docs.** `docs/decks.md` says the slide is the shape 16:9, drawn at the size of its room with every size a fraction of the slide, and prints on a page of 960 by 540 pt (13.33 by 7.5 inches); its lines 13, 74, 92 and 105 are rewritten so nothing names a frame size or a deck key for it. `docs/templates.md:1929` loses the commented `frame:` line. Every comment in the changed files that names the frame's px or a px box (`1150 px`, `1040 px`, "the frame's own px"), `deck.frame` or `defaults.frame` is rewritten (verify 4).
10. **The guarded entry.** The `frameSetting` tests leave `settings.test.ts`; G13's line naming them (`agents/guarded.md:257`) is rewritten by the orchestrator at landing, since a builder does not edit the ledger. The report gives the line's replacement under "Doc text".

## Decisions taken in this brief (each reversible, none silent)

1. **The conversion keeps today's look.** A px value N of the 1280-wide frame becomes N/1280 of the frame's width, so a slide drawn at any size looks as it does today at 1280 by 720 scaled. Five real cases from the tree: `--slide-design-title: 56px` gives `4.375cqw`; `--slide-design-body: 24px` gives `1.875cqw`; `padding: 64px` gives `5cqw`; `--slide-reading: 1040px` gives `81.25cqw`; `--fs-xs` at 11 px on a slide gives `0.859375cqw`. In `slide-layout.ts`, `TEXT_LEAST = 360` gives 0.28125 and `TEXT_GAP = 26` gives 0.0203125 of the frame's width.
2. **Hairlines stay 1px.** A 1px border or rule inside a slide stays 1px, since it draws one device pixel and sizes nothing.
3. **The PDF page is 960 by 540 pt**, the page a 1280 by 720 frame prints at today, so an exported PDF keeps its page size.
4. **The too-tall finding gives the overrun in percent of the frame's height**, a whole number of at least 1 (the dictated line).
5. **The rice sizes are 12/13 of the default fractions**, unrounded, and the rice chip and table steps keep today's 10, 11, 12 of 1280.
6. **The browser's audience window opens at half the screen's available width, 16:9**, since a window needs a size and the slide gives none.
7. **The chip, the tag and the table change only inside a slide**, through a rule scoped to the slide frame, so the app's other views keep their px.
