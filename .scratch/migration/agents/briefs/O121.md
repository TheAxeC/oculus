## What to build

1. **One element for a record card's controls.** The strip and the "..." opener become one element, so they move together. `CardActions.svelte` takes the opener as a snippet prop and draws it last inside its `data-card-control` span; `RecordCard.svelte` hands it the `Menu`. `.menu` and its `data-card-control` go; the opener keeps its look. `measure` takes the opener's width and the strip's gap both into the least the strip needs (`least`) and out of the room given to `fitActions`, since the opener no longer counts among `others`. A strip with no action needs the opener alone, with no gap. The controls that do not fit still go into More by `fitActions`; a room at or below zero after the opener is taken means every action goes into More, which `measure` guards, since `fitActions` reads such a room as not measured.
2. **Controls that do not fit lie over the card.** A class on the span, set by `measure`, never a redraw by `{#if}`, so focus stays on a control. With the class, on a screen that hovers, the span lies over the face at its top right corner, `top: 8px; right: 12px`, painted `var(--card)`, as a row with no chip draws them. The class stands when `measure` finds no room beside the chips (`place.alone`) and the row holds chips; a row with no chip is placed by `Card.svelte`'s rule alone and never takes the class, so the two offsets do not add. A card clipped by a narrower pane keeps the span at the visible right edge, by `right` raised by the measured clipped width, so it can be pointed at and the title's start stays pressable. The class stands from the start until a measurement says otherwise, so a span not yet measured cannot wrap. `measure` also runs at rest from the remembered widths once they are known, so a narrowed pane updates the class with no draw. The chips stay in the flow.
3. **Controls that fit stay where they are.** A row with room keeps them at the line's end beside the chips, as now.
4. **Hovering moves nothing, from the first frame.** At every card width, the card's height is the same at rest, under the pointer, with focus inside, with a menu open and under the keyboard cursor, and no frame is painted with the card grown. A screen that does not hover keeps its row in the flow, where the controls are drawn at rest and nothing jumps.
5. **The Usage card.** `.act` in `Usage.svelte`'s own style, on a screen that hovers, lies over the face's top right corner in every state, painted `var(--card)`, so the row of Tags never grows. It takes no `data-card-control`, since its row always holds a Tag. It stays held drawn while a press waits.
6. **Comments and pages.** The head comments of `Card.svelte`, `CardActions.svelte`, `RecordCard.svelte` and `Usage.svelte`, the comment on `--card-at-rest`, the comments at `Card.svelte:220` and `Card.svelte:273-282`, the comment at `CardActions.svelte:203-205`, the comments on `.act` in `Usage.svelte`, the comments of `card-layout.ts:36-48` and `docs/HELP.md:301` say what is true after the change. `gap: 0` in `Card.svelte`'s rule goes, since the row now has one child. A comment stating that the controls take a line of their own goes or changes. `card-layout.ts` changes in its comments only.

## Cases

- A card with one chip and room: controls at the line's end beside the chip, height unchanged.
- A card too narrow for chips and controls on one line: controls over the top right corner, chips untouched, height unchanged at rest, under the pointer, with focus inside, with a menu open and under the keyboard cursor.
- A row of chips that wraps to two lines: as the case above.
- The first hover after a load and after a pane is narrowed: sampled per animation frame, no frame shows a greater height.
- A pane widened while the pointer is on a covered card: the controls go back beside the chips, and the card's height equals its height at rest at the new width.
- A pane narrower than its card (a clipped card) with chips: the controls lie at the top left where they can be pointed at, and the height does not change.
- A card with no chip: as O120 left it, over the top right corner, with the 6px gap of the span now standing between the last action and "...".
- A card with no chip narrower than its controls: placed by `Card.svelte`'s rule alone, with no second offset.
- A card with a "..." and no strip action: the opener alone at the line's end.
- A visible line under about 27 px: every action in More, the opener last.
- A card narrower than More and "..." together: the span passes the card's left edge and stays reachable from its right end; nothing moves.
- Focus on a control while a pane is resized across the place the class changes: the focus stays.
- A card with three strip actions, at a width where they do not fit: the actions that do not fit go into More, the opener stays last, and the strip does not pass the card's edge.
- A menu opened from "..." on a covered card: it stays by its opener and is not cut off.
- A right-click and the context menu key on a control: the card's menu, as now.
- Tab through a card's controls: the order is the strip, then "...", as now.
- A screen that does not hover: read from the rules, since the look's pointer hovers: the class's styles and Usage's stand only under `@media (hover: hover)`, so the row stays in the flow and nothing is covered.
- A wallpaper theme and a rice theme: the span's box is solid and is all that is painted.
- The Usage card at a narrow width: Tags untouched, height unchanged under the pointer; a pressed refresh stays drawn, turning, and goes back to the card's rule on its answer.

## Decisions taken in this brief (each reversible, none silent)

1. Controls that do not fit lie over the title's corner, not over the chips. A chip can be a control, and covering one under the pointer takes it away; the title's corner is the place ruled for a card with no chip.
2. Controls that fit stay beside the chips. Moving every card's controls to the corner would be simpler, and would change every card to end a case most cards never meet.
3. The Usage card's control always lies in the corner. It has no measuring code, and adding some for one control is more code than the case needs.
4. A long title's corner is covered under the pointer, as ruled for a card with no chip.
5. A clipped card keeps the controls at the visible right edge, so the title's start stays pressable.
6. Beside a span with one element, the 6px gap stands between the last action and "...", painted solid.
