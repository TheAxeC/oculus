## What to build

1. **The lines after a pane move** (diagnosis 2). `subscribePty` takes the grid's size as a function it reads when it needs it: `subscribePty(id, size: () => { cols: number; rows: number }, onBytes)`. It listens on the channel at once and posts `resizeTerminal(id, cols, rows)` with `size()`. Once that resize has answered, whatever its answer, it reads `size()` again: when the grid's size changed while the resize was in flight, it posts the new size and waits for that answer too; when it did not, it subscribes to the channel. A resize that has not answered within `RESIZE_WAIT` (an exported constant of 2000 ms beside the function) counts as answered, so a request that never returns never leaves the grid blank. The function returned takes the listener off, and a listener taken off before the subscribe asks for nothing more (no later resize, no subscribe). Resizes of one terminal are posted one after another: a second `subscribePty` for the same id, while the first one's resize is in flight (a remount, two quick moves), posts its resize only after the first one's has answered, so the window ends at the latest grid's size. Input `sendInput` is given for a terminal whose subscribe is waiting on its resize is held and sent, in order, right after the subscribe, so keys typed in that round trip are not dropped by the server; input for a listener taken off is dropped. Its doc comment says why: the scrollback is captured at the window's size with the cursor placed from the pane's top. `TerminalGrid.svelte`'s mount passes `() => ({ cols: term.cols, rows: term.rows })`, read after `fit.fit()`, and drops its own `resized(wanted)` right after the subscribe. A grid of no size at mount (a hidden pane, where `fit.fit()` leaves xterm's 80x24) posts xterm's size, as `resized()` does today; its `ResizeObserver` sends the real size once the pane has one. The type at `terminals.svelte.ts:175` follows. The existing subscribe tests in `tests/state/sessions/terminals.test.ts` take the size and stub the resize's fetch.
2. **The watchers' debounce with a maximum wait** (diagnosis 3, Z135 (a), Z136 corrected to (b)). Add `lodash-es` to `dependencies` and `@types/lodash-es` to `devDependencies`, and remove `perfect-debounce`. Each of the five watchers uses `debounce` from `lodash-es` with its quiet as `wait` and `{ maxWait }`, where `maxWait` is an exported constant beside the quiet at twice the module's own quiet (`MAX_WAIT = 2 * DEBOUNCE` in `sessions.ts`, `bookmarks.ts`, `portfolio.ts` and `local.ts`, `USAGE_MAX_WAIT = 2 * USAGE_DEBOUNCE` in `usage/watch.ts`); `portfolio.ts` and `local.ts` export their `DEBOUNCE`. The constant is not derived from an injected `options.debounce`: an injected quiet of 0 keeps the module's `maxWait`, and lodash raises `maxWait` to at least the wait, so an injected quiet longer than the constant (`local.test.ts:64` injects 1500) waits its quiet. A burst that ends within one quiet is one rebuild; under events that never stop, a rebuild runs at least every `maxWait`, on lodash's own timer. `cancel()` on close and each `closed` guard stay. The comments that explain the wait are rewritten true to the new code: the heads at `sessions.ts:7`, `local.ts:9` and `usage/watch.ts:5-7`, and the doc comment of `bookmarks.ts:34`, each name lodash's `debounce`, the maximum wait and why (a writer more frequent than the quiet would otherwise hold the channel back for as long as it writes); `usage/watch.ts` says what happens to a rebuild still running when the next one fires, read from its code; `portfolio.ts`'s doc comment of its constants says the same. Tests fake `Date` with the timers (`vi.useFakeTimers({ toFake: ['setTimeout', 'clearTimeout', 'Date'] })`), since lodash reads `Date.now()`, and import the module's constants; `local.test.ts` and `bookmarks.test.ts` keep their own injected `DEBOUNCE = 50` for their existing cases and use the module's constants for the new ones. The cases 5 to 11 below say what each watcher's tests assert. `sessions.ts` carries the cases of the diagnosis record (a started terminal under writes every 100, 300 and 450 ms; a renamed terminal with a new icon and colour; the session's own appended prompts), quoted there in full under "Fix and test".
3. **A sole terminal's menu** (ruling "Q109 rename"). A right-click on the bar's name of a sole terminal (`TerminalTitle.svelte`, shown while no list is drawn) opens, through the same `ContextMenu` the list's rows use, the menu a row of the list opens, `terminalRowMenu(tab, shown, others, arming === shown, t)`, and every pick does what the same pick does on that row: Split Terminal, Kill Terminal (arming first), Rename (opens the bar's rename field), Change Icon, Change Color, Clear, and Join Terminals when another tab owns a terminal; Unsplit Terminal is not offered, since `terminalRowMenu` offers it only on a row of more than one. While the bar's rename field is open, a right-click on it is the field's own (the browser's text menu), as in the list's rename field. Kill armed from the bar is disarmed as the grid's menu's arming is (`Terminal.svelte:211-218`, a terminal joining or leaving). The pick routing is one function both places call: move `rowPicked`'s dispatch out of `TerminalList.svelte` into a pure function in `terminal-list.ts` that takes the pick and the handlers, and have the list and the bar call it; Join's state change goes where both can reach it. Its tests go in `tests/views/sessions/terminal-menu.test.ts`. `HELP.md:127` names the bar's right-click menu among the terminal's menus.
4. **Four terminal settings** (D30, Z82 (a), Z137 (a)). Under `terminal:` in `config/appearance.yml`, beside `font_px`, four keys whose values are today's behaviour, each with a one-line comment:
   - `mac_option_is_meta: false`: true makes the Option key send Meta (xterm's `macOptionIsMeta`).
   - `copy_on_selection: false`: true copies a selection to the clipboard as it changes (the grid's `onSelectionChange`, writing `getSelection()` when it is not empty). Select All from the grid's menu is a selection change and copies the whole buffer. Turning the key on while a selection exists copies nothing until the selection changes. A clipboard that refuses, or a page with no `navigator.clipboard` (where the call throws at once), copies nothing and throws nothing.
   - `cursor_style: block`: `block`, `line` or `underline`; `line` is xterm's `bar`.
   - `cursor_blinking: false`: true makes the cursor blink (xterm's `cursorBlink`).
   `appearance.ts` reads them beside `readTerminalFont` and hands them out as one read, `terminalOptions()`, which calls `configGeneration()` as `terminalFontPx()` does. Under `spec.md:48` (a value with a config key gets no fallback in code beside it), a key that is absent, null, empty or of the wrong type (`cursor_blinking: "yes"`, `mac_option_is_meta: 1`, `cursor_style: ""`, `cursor_style: beam`), or a `terminal:` that is not a map, is left out of the read, and the xterm option is left unset, so xterm's own default applies (block, no blink, Option not Meta) and copy on selection is off. `gridOptions` takes the read into the options a grid is built with, as an optional parameter, so `terminal.test.ts:433` and `:442` stand. A mounted grid follows a config change through an effect of its own in `TerminalGrid.svelte`, which reads `terminalOptions()` in its body and sets the three xterm options and the copy-on-selection listener through one exported function of `grid-look.ts` (`followTerminalOptions(term, options)` or a name of the file's kind); a key it reads as unset sets the option back to xterm's default. That effect sets only these options: it calls no `refit` and no `resized`, so a push of any config file sends no resize. Four catalog entries in `config/catalogs/options.yml` after `terminal.font_px`, each with a description true to its reader, its default and its control (`toggle`, `toggle`, `choice` with `values: [block, line, underline]`, `toggle`). `HELP.md:221` names the four. The reader's tests go beside the `terminalFontPx` tests in `tests/ui/theme/theme.test.ts`; `tones.test.ts:224` asserts the five keys.
5. **Docs**: `DESIGN.md:116` names lodash's `debounce` with its maximum wait in place of perfect-debounce's, and says why in one sentence; the clause about an async rebuild is rewritten to what the code does after the change, or removed when nothing in it is true. `README.md:13` names `lodash-es` (MIT) in place of `perfect-debounce`.

1. The four keys' controls are their catalog entries' controls, which S3c's editor draws; this step builds no Settings control of its own (the premise correction above; S3c's line names the keys).
2. `maxWait` is twice each watcher's own quiet, as diagnosis 3 measured for `sessions.ts`, a module constant that an injected quiet does not change: a burst that ends within one quiet stays one rebuild, and steady writes publish at least once per `maxWait`.
3. A clipboard that refuses a copy-on-selection write, or is missing, is ignored, not said: the selection is still there to copy by hand, and a notice at every selection would be noise.
4. A terminal key that is absent or not one of its values leaves its xterm option unset, so the only defaults are the file's values and xterm's own (`spec.md:48`).
5. The pick routing of a row's menu is one pure function in `terminal-list.ts`, so the bar and the list cannot drift apart.
6. `RESIZE_WAIT` is 2000 ms: a tmux `resize-window` answers in milliseconds, and a grid left blank longer than two seconds reads as broken.
7. Input typed while the subscribe waits on its resize is held and sent after the subscribe, so moving a tab never loses keys.
8. A grid of no size at mount posts xterm's size, as today; its `ResizeObserver` corrects it when the pane gets a size.
