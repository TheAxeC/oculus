## What is on the tree (read on main at e3e50a421)

The sessions rescan:

- `watchSessions` (`src/lib/server/root/watch/sessions.ts`, 325 lines) calls `sessions()` (`src/lib/server/sessions/index.ts`) on the server's one thread: at start, at each debounced file event (DEBOUNCE 500 ms, MAX_WAIT 1000 ms), in `recheck` every 15 s and in `sweep` every 5 min. `rescan` publishes the whole payload every time, changed or not (`sed -n 180,245p`).
- `sessions()` runs `scan()` (`stores/transcripts.ts`), then `mergeHooks` (`stores/hooks.ts`, every record a new object through `{ ...record, hook }`), then `group()` (`group.ts`, every grouped session a copy of its record with `workspace`, `area`, `secondaryAreas`, `kind`, `pinned`, `top`, and `title` replaced when the overrides file names one).

## What to build

Part 1, the server thread.

1. **The sessions scan runs in a worker thread.** Reading the stores, `ps`, the cache file and `group()` all run in a worker held by `watchSessions`; the server's thread does none of them, at start, at a file event, at the 15 s re-check or at the 5 min clock. `sessions()` stays as it is for its other callers (the tests among them). What the worker needs (the folders, the roots, the project folders as the portfolio has them at that moment, the cache and overrides paths, the window and the cap) goes with each request; the hook store and the terminals stay on the server's thread, and hooks are merged and the terminals added there when the answer comes, so a hook that arrives while a scan runs is on the published record. The records as scanned are kept apart from the records as published: a scan's state replaces what a hook's `patchRecord` put on a published record at the next rescan, as now, and whether a rescan changed anything is judged against what stands on the channel, never against the worker's last answer alone.
   - At most one scan runs and one waits; a request while one waits joins it, so writes that never stop cost one scan at a time. A file event during a scan starts another one after it. A request that joins a waiting one of another kind takes the stronger rule: a rescan over the 5 min clock, and either over the 15 s re-check.
   - What the watcher publishes, when, and what it tells `onChange` listeners stay as now, but for a rescan that changed nothing: it publishes nothing and tells nobody, as the 5 min clock already does. The first publish comes when the first scan answers.
   - A scan that fails is one line on stderr and the previous payload stands. A worker that dies fails the scan that ran, with one line, and the next request starts another, whose first answer holds every record: a record whose content is what the server holds keeps its object, and nothing is published for it. A record a re-check read as changed and did not publish is published by the next rescan. While a watcher is closed and another started for a changed `accounts` key, the cache file stays one whole, atomic file. `close` ends the worker, and nothing is published after it. The worker never keeps the process up.
   - `probe` and `codexProbe` leave `SessionsWatchOptions`: no caller gives the watcher either (`grep -rn watchSessions tests` finds `inert-context.ts` alone). `sessions()` keeps its own, for its tests; the worker composes the scan from the same functions.
   - In the worker, `readerDigest` is computed once and the cache file is written only when a scan changed an entry. Writing it stays atomic.
2. **The channel sends what changed.** A page that subscribes is sent the whole payload, as now. From then on a message holds only the records that changed (new or different), the ids in order, and the tree; a session in the tree is its placement only, with no record repeated, and the tree is left out when it did not change. Unchanged records cost no comparison by text: the worker answers only the records whose content changed, the server keeps the other record objects as they are, and the records to send are found by identity. The tree is compared by its placements, since a hook makes every workspace and area object new. `hub.latest('sessions')` stays the whole payload, so every reader inside the server reads it as now.
   - A page that falls behind is sent the difference from the last message it was sent to the newest state, never from one it was not sent.
   - The wire shape is under "Decisions taken in this brief" 1. The page applies it to what it holds and hands the rest of the page the same payload object shape it has now, with the unchanged records the same objects, so no reader of `entities.sessions()` changes.
   - A message whose base the page does not hold is not applied: the page asks the channel again (`subscribe(['sessions'], true)`) and is sent the whole payload. It asks once until the whole payload came, and a whole payload that fails the page's shape is dropped as a malformed frame is, with no further ask before the next whole payload or reconnect.
   - The join of records and placements into the payload is one pure function that the server and the page both call, in `src/lib/data/model/model-sessions.ts` and exported through `index.ts` (872 lines of 880).
   - A publish of a hook (`root/hooks.ts`) goes out as a difference as well.
   - The server's two sinks on the `sessions` channel (`git.ts` for its three channels, `schedules/watch.ts`) are told with no text made: the Hub hands an in-process reader the message itself, the latest at its subscribe included, and `git.ts` parses no text. The four `config` sinks (`lsp/held/watch.ts`, `watch/apps.ts`, `live-settings.ts`, `files/fs/open-files.ts`) stay as they are. `git.ts` stays at or under 880 lines.
   - The mechanism is the Hub's, per channel, registered as `merge` is; a channel with none behaves as now.
3. **Keys go through the control client.** Typing into a terminal goes to the control client of that terminal when one is attached, and to a `tmux` process when none is, through one method of `TerminalDriver`. Order per terminal holds (`#chain`), across the switch between the two ways included. The pieces stay `inputArgs`'s. A piece that fails after it was written is never sent again by the other way, since whether the pane took it is not known; it is the same one line on stderr as now. A write to a control client that has ended, or whose `close` was called in the same turn, is that command's failure and never an `error` event nobody handles, which would end the server (`tmux.ts` gives `child.stdin` no `error` listener). A key whose reply has not come after `TIMEOUT_MS` is one stderr line, is not sent again, and the next key goes on. Resizes, steers and every other command stay as they are.
4. **The usage fold reads what was appended, in a worker thread.** A transcript is read from the byte after its last complete line, with what the fold carried (the Codex model, the running totals, the rows), and from zero when the file shrank, its inode changed, or its mtime moved with its size as it was. A rewrite in place that grew is read as an append, as the sessions scan reads it. The rows a fold answers are the rows a whole read of the same bytes by this reader gives; the reader is `foldFrom`'s or `foldYielding`'s, so a record holding U+2028, which `readline` splits and drops, is counted, and the report lists that change. The listing of the stores and the reads run in a worker of its own, so a long first read holds neither the server nor the sessions scan; `usageAccounts` stays on the server's thread. `UsageTranscriptFold` stays the class the test calls. A line with no newline at its end waits for the next fold.
   - The rules of the sessions worker for one running and one waiting, failure, death and `close` hold here too, and are written once for both, in `src/lib/server/machine/threads/` (a held worker with its requests, taking its worker file as an option as `HeldComments` does), reusing nothing of `comments.ts` unless the two fit one shape; the report says which. A fold that ends after `close` publishes nothing.

Part 2, Present.

5. **A slide's layout is kept.** What `layOutSlide` settles on (the factor, the room, the layout, the boxes, the failed figures and the depth) is kept by the deck's root and path with the slide's key (as `tallKey` of `present.svelte.ts:439`) and by what it was made from: the slide and its strip as drawn, the probe's size as a fraction of the frame (`PROBE_DRIFT` holds the rounding), the theme's body classes and the text scale, the configuration's generation (`words/generation.svelte.ts`), how often fonts have finished loading, and the state and natural size of each of its images. A font that finishes loading while a neighbour is prepared therefore has the shown slide laid out again, as now. A slide asked to lay itself out again with the same inputs puts the kept layout on and tries no factor; one with other inputs searches and replaces the kept layout. A kept layout puts nothing on a slide whose images are not in the state it was made with, and a layout made with fewer images loaded does not replace one made with all of them loaded. Every formula is fitted (`fitFormulas`) when a kept layout is put on, since the search leaves its sizes on the drawn elements. The kept layouts hold one entry per slide key and go when their deck does. They are held by the client's Present store for the workbench and by the page for the audience window; nothing is held in a module. The types of a kept layout (`Layout`, `Box`) move to `src/lib/state/life/present.ts` (739 lines), which `slide-layout.ts` imports, since the state layer imports no view.
   - The pane, the presenter screen, the Overview and the audience window read and fill it; the print page does not need it.
   - The layout modules of `screen/` (`slide-layout.ts`, `drawn-slide.ts`, `layout-turns.ts` and the new ones) move into a folder of `screen/`, so no folder passes ten; their importers are all inside `present/`. `layOutSlide` moves to a module there (its search and its state as it is now, not shortened to fit), so `Slide.svelte` stays at or under 520 lines.
6. **The slides on either side are laid out while the page is idle.** The pane, the presenter screen and the audience window, once their slide has been laid out, lay out the slide after it and then the one before it, at the same frame size, in a Stage nobody sees or reaches (hidden by visibility, `inert`, `aria-hidden`, out of the tab order and of Cmd+F, taking no pointer, and never the container the scroll keys take: `scrollContainer`, `shell/keys/key-dom.ts:86`, takes the first `[data-scroll]`), so their images are loaded and their layouts kept before they are shown. The work runs in idle time, one factor of the search at a time, and gives way to a key press between two factors; a switch of slide drops what is left of it. It reports no measure to the Present store and writes nothing. It prepares nothing while the tab is hidden or the Overview is shown, and nothing on the print page. In the presenter screen, where the next slide is already drawn, only the slides not drawn are prepared.
   - A layout that is not prepared in time is searched as now.

Part 3, the emails.

7. **The line unfolds.** In Focus's Needs attention group, the line of emails has a fold control before it, drawn as the fold controls of Focus's headings are (`FoldControl`, named with the line's words), shown only when the line is. It is folded at the start, also on a tab whose state names no fold, and its fold is the Focus tab's, in a list of its own, `opened`, beside `closed` in the tab's state, holding `emails` while the fold is open, so a fold that starts closed has its own rule and `closed` keeps its meaning and its keys. A wrong `opened` value names none.
   - Unfolded, the line is followed by the threads the count counts, in three groups in the order reply, act on, not yet sorted, under the headings `inbox.sec_reply`, `inbox.sec_action` and `inbox.sec_unsorted`; a group with no thread is not drawn. A thread is one row: its name as `InboxCard` names it, its subject as the muted detail, its age as the tail in `InboxCard`'s words (nothing when it cannot be read). A thread whose name is its subject has no detail. The order in a group is the plate's.
   - A press on a thread opens the Inbox at its card (`reveal` with `search: ''`); a press on the line opens the Inbox as now. There is no other control on the row.
   - The naming and age words of `InboxCard` are read from one place by both.
   - A sender or a subject is drawn as the characters it holds, never as Markdown or HTML; an address in a subject is not a link, as `InboxCard` draws it.
   - The rows follow a publish of the inbox while unfolded: a thread triaged elsewhere leaves them.
   - `TickerRow` and `Ticker` gain the fold by their contract, the control itself drawn by a snippet the caller gives, as `marked` is, since this folder imports nothing of `ui/controls`; the Gallery's sample shows it. A cut row's hover words hold its detail as well (`reveal.svelte.ts` `whole`). A thread that leaves or joins the list does not take the keyboard from another thread's row: the list's key no longer holds the place of a row whose id is its own.

The words of every new or changed catalog key go in the group its view's words are in.

## Decisions taken in this brief (each reversible, none silent)

1. The wire shape of a difference. The message keeps `channel` and `version`; its payload is `{ base, order, records, placed?, terminals? }`: `base` the version of the message the page was last sent, `order` the path of every record in order (a path, since two stores can hold one id), `records` the records that changed, whole, `placed` the tree when it changed, each session in it `{ path, workspace, area, secondaryAreas, kind, pinned, top, title }` and no more, with no `lastActivity` on an area or a workspace, which both ends compute from the sessions as `group.ts` does, `terminals` the list when it changed. A whole payload is the shape as now. The page tells the two apart by `base`. Worked on the real payload, 40 records: one record of 24 KB changed gives a message of about 30 KB (the order is about 4 KB) in place of 989 KB; a session added gives its record, the order and the tree; a session renamed by an override gives no record and the tree; a hook gives one record; a terminal renamed gives the terminals alone. The figures are estimates by proportion, and the report gives the measured ones.
2. The worker is per server and per use (one for the scan, one for the usage fold), started at first need, and ended by `close`.
3. The kept layouts are memory only, one entry per slide key, and nothing of them is saved. Saving them would be a new form of stored data for a gain the idle preparation already gives.
4. The usage fold keeps its state in memory only: after a start of the server the first fold reads the week once more, in its own thread. A stored state would be a new file under `~/.oculus`.
5. The last line of a transcript with no newline is counted when its newline comes. A file that ends without one for good has its last record left out.
6. A rescan that changed nothing publishes nothing and tells no listener. The usage rebuild then runs by its own clock, which folds once a minute, and by the next change.
7. The fold of the emails is kept as `opened: ['emails']` in the Focus tab's state, beside `closed`.
8. The print page neither reads nor fills the kept layouts: it draws each slide once.
