## What to build

1. **The status bar scrolls sideways.** When the items of the bar do not all fit, every item stays drawn and the bar scrolls left and right: a trackpad's sideways swipe and a mouse wheel's turn both scroll it (a wheel's vertical motion moves the bar sideways, since the bar has nothing to scroll vertically), and moving the focus to an item with Tab scrolls that item into view. The wheel's turn goes through `horizontalWheelDelta` (`tab-strip.ts:173`), the rule the tab strip uses, not a second one. A sideways swipe that reaches either end of the bar stops there and never reaches Chrome's back gesture (`overscroll-behavior-x: contain` on the bar). No scrollbar is drawn, Chrome's thin scrollbars included, so the bar keeps its 22 px. An item that appears or leaves while the bar is scrolled keeps the scroll position, clamped to the new width; a resize that makes everything fit draws the bar as today, and one that makes it overflow opens it at its left end. A menu or tooltip opened from an item anchors to the item where it is drawn. The debug colouring and the wallpaper ground fill the whole visible bar at any scroll position, and app items scroll with the rest in their order. When everything fits, the bar draws as today: the left group at the left, the right group's items from the window's right edge with the bell last at the edge. When it does not fit, the left group starts at the bar's left edge, the right group follows it, and the bar opens scrolled to its left end. `one-row.ts` is deleted, and `leftOut` and `DROP_ORDER` leave `status-items.ts`, with every sentence that names them or that the change makes false (the head comment of `StatusBar.svelte`, its CSS comments `:356-358` and `:378-381`, the doc comment of `DROP_ORDER`, `docs/HELP.md:122`). An item keeps its cap of 40 % of the window and its ellipsis. Hiding an item from the right-click menu works as today.
2. **The nine languages' settings.** `registerSettings` gives Monaco the settings of `latex` and `bibtex` as LaTeX Workshop `v10.19.0`'s two files give them, of `shellscript`, `make`, `diff` and `julia` as VS Code's own files give them (`julia` because the table test of item 3, which the step's line orders, needs a comment rule for every language the editor registers), each field the file has that Monaco's `LanguageConfiguration` takes (comments, brackets, auto-closing pairs, surrounding pairs, indentation rules, folding markers, `autoCloseBefore`, the word pattern), of `matlab` as MathWorks' `language-configuration.json` gives it and of `ocaml` as the OCaml Platform's `languages/ocaml.json` gives it (the files VS Code users take for those languages), and of `common-lisp` (line comment `;`, block comment `#|` `|#`, the three bracket pairs, their auto-closing and surrounding pairs, and `"`). Make's line comment is given whole as `{ comment: '#', noIndent: true }`. The files' JSON is turned into Monaco's shape by the existing reader `languageConfiguration` (`kit/languages/configuration.ts`), which this step extends to keep a `lineComment` object's `noIndent` and to read `autoCloseBefore`, so one reader serves app languages and these. The LaTeX Workshop settings live in a new file `src/lib/ui/controls/editor/kit/rite/latex-settings.ts` whose head comment names LaTeX Workshop `v10.19.0`, its two files, the MIT License and Copyright (c) 2016 James Yu; `settings.ts`'s head comment lists every language it configures. Where a LaTeX Workshop auto-closing pair conflicts with the LaTeX tab's own editing (its environment completion of `\begin{`, its Enter in a list), the tab's own editing keeps working and the report names how, read in `latex-editing.ts:179-198` and `latex-suggest.ts:48`, with the look as proof: taking the `@(`, `@[` or `@{` suggestion (`config/catalogs/latex.yml:69-79`) leaves no stray closing bracket. The visible changes the new settings bring (the line after `\begin{x}` indented and `\end{` outdented, the word that double-click, Cmd+D and word moves take on `\textbf{word}`, a `{` left unclosed after `\begin`, `\left` or `\bigl`) are listed in the report's before and after.
3. **A table test that every language the editor registers has a comment rule** (ruling 250). One table, one row per id of `SHIKI_LANGUAGES` and `rite`: the row passes when `registerSettings` gives the id a `comments` field, or when Monaco's own `conf` for the id (imported from its `definitions/<id>/<id>.js`, or `features/json/jsonMode.js` for json) has one, so the row reads Monaco's data rather than a list in the test and follows a Monaco upgrade. The test fails today for nine ids, the eight of the line and `julia`. It is the test of a new guarded entry G121, worded in the report's "Doc text" for the orchestrator.
4. **Every default key Monaco binds, against what oculus takes.** The builder lists every key binding Monaco 0.57 registers by default, read from `node_modules/monaco-editor` (each `kbOpts`, each `registerCommandAndKeybindingRule`, each `KeybindingsRegistry.registerKeybindingRule` and each Action2 `keybinding:` property, with its `primary`, `mac` and `when`), checked for completeness against the registry `monaco-keys.ts` reads at run time, as a table in the report: the press on macOS, the command, its `when` and the state the look puts the editor in to meet it (a selection, the suggest widget open, the find widget focused, snippet mode), and for a `.tex`, a `.slurm` and a `.yml` source tab whether the press reaches that command, and if not what takes it (a freed key, a LaTeX press, a page key of the workbench's tables, a language setting the command needs that the language lacks, a language server not running, Chrome itself, the desktop app's menu). A row whose `when` a source tab never meets (`inDiffEditor` for F7 and Shift+F7, reference peek, accessibility help) says so and is not a failure; a key Chrome reserves (Cmd+W, Cmd+T, Cmd+N, Cmd+Shift+T, Cmd+Q) or the app's menu takes is named and is not a failure where VS Code's own window takes it too. Each press that does not reach its command where VS Code's would (VS Code with LaTeX Workshop for the `.tex` row) is fixed at its cause in this step. F1, F8 and Shift+F8 stay the workbench's, as VS Code's workbench takes them. Cmd+K, its chords and Ctrl+K on macOS follow Axel's ruling on open item 258. A row that fails at the look is fixed at landing or in a repair round, by the rules file's "Nothing left undone". This table is what the orchestrator presses through in Chrome at the look.
5. **The LaTeX keys against LaTeX Workshop.** In a LaTeX tab, Cmd+L then Cmd+L selects the line (Monaco's `expandLineSelection`) and Cmd+L then Ctrl+Shift+M toggles tab focus mode (Monaco's `editor.action.toggleTabFocusMode`), as LaTeX Workshop binds them; both are new second presses in `LATEX_CHORD_SECONDS`, which becomes platform-dependent as `latexMathFirst` is, so off macOS tab focus mode is Ctrl+L then Ctrl+M as LaTeX Workshop binds it; bound in `latex-tab.svelte.ts`, listed in `help/keys/rows.ts` and in `docs/HELP.md`'s key table (`:168`), with their words as new keys beside `cmd_item` and `cmd_surround` in `config/text/views/academic/latex.yml`. Cmd+Alt+[ and ] stay promote and demote, as LaTeX Workshop binds them. The chords follow LaTeX Workshop's scopes: in a `.bib` file the text and math chords apply and the two new chords do not; in a `.sty` or `.cls` file no text chord and no Cmd+L Cmd+L applies, so Cmd+L selects the line as in any other file. A configured text chord whose second press is one of the chord seconds (`l`, `w`, Enter, through `latexChordSecond`, `editor-keys.ts:37-47`) is left out and named, as the LaTeX tables leave out a row they cannot use.
