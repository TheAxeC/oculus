## What to build

1. **A line's place.** `foldPieces` hands `apply` the byte the line starts at as well as the record, and takes an end: it reads no byte at or past `until` when one is given. `foldYielding` passes `until` on through `YieldingRead`. `foldFrom` and the scan are unchanged in what they do. `readTurns` notes each turn's line while it reads, and that note does not reach the wire.
2. **A read's start.** A read through `handleTranscript` that ends at a byte answers `head`: the byte of the line of the oldest turn it keeps, or 0 when nothing older than those turns can hold a turn because the read reached the file's head and kept all it read. A slice never cuts between turns of one line: the turns of the oldest kept line are all kept, so a read may answer a few more than `FIRST_READ_TURNS`. The first read answers `head`. An outcome of a call in a dropped turn with a non-empty id goes in the answer's `results`, so a later read of that call can draw it; a Codex turn carries its own outcome, so nothing of Codex goes there. The line note never reaches the wire: the route names the fields it answers, so a `from` answer holds exactly `turns`, `offset` and `results`, and a first answer those and `head`.
3. **A read before a byte.** `GET /api/transcript?id=...&before=<byte>`, optional `agent`, answers `{ turns, head, results }` with the same shape of work as a first read and one function shared with it: it reads the last `FIRST_READ_BYTES` before `before`, doubling while that holds no turn, keeps the last `FIRST_READ_TURNS`, and renders only those. A `before` that is not a non-negative integer is refused with 400 as `from` is, and an empty `before` reads as absent. With both, `before` wins. `before=0` answers no turn and `head` 0, reading nothing. A `before` past the file's size reads to the size. `format=markdown` wins over `before`, as it does over `from`. `from` is unchanged and its answer holds no `head`.
4. **The store.** `TurnsState` gains `head` (the byte to ask before, 0 when there is nothing older), `results` (every outcome the reads gave, kept), `above` (how many turns reads of older turns have added at the top), `older` (whether such a read is out) and `olderError` (what the last one failed with, or the empty text). `readOlder(id, agent)` on the store asks once: a second call while one is out, or with `head` 0, does nothing. Its answer is prepended to the turns held when it lands, with `results` applied to the new turns, so a call reads its outcome whichever read saw it first. A tail read landing while an older read is out, or the other way round, loses neither's turns: each merges into what is held when it lands, and `tail` no longer writes over a copy taken at its start. An older answer is applied only when the held `head` still equals the `before` it asked with, so an answer that lands after `forget`, or after a new first read, holds nothing. An answer of `from` 0 sets `head` and `results` from its body, `above` 0 and `older` false; an answer of `from` above 0 keeps the held `head` and adds its `results`. The failure paths of `tail` stop writing the copy taken at the start as well. A failed read keeps the turns and sets `olderError`. Hold it through `queries.peek` and `queries.hold`, and never through `queries.refetch`, which would replace the fetcher that a mark asks with.
5. **Rows keep their keys.** `groupTurns` counts `first` from the top of the list as it stood at the first read, so a row's index subtracts `above` and is negative for an older row. `TranscriptBody` keys rows by it and `runKey` follows, so a pressed run keeps its choice when turns come in above and a row is not drawn anew. When a read extends the run at the top of the list, that run's choice moves to its new key. `turnDocs` keys on `turn.html` in place of the turn object, since a prepend gives held turns new objects, so an answer's document and its Copy controls stand across a read, and `turn-rows.ts:6-16` says so.
6. **The view.** When the reader is within one column height of the top of `.chat` and `head` is above 0, `TranscriptBody` calls `readOlder`. This is tested on a scroll and after each draw, so a list shorter than the pane fills it, with these limits: the after-draw test runs after the send to the foot of a first draw or a change of session, so opening a list taller than two columns reads nothing older; nothing asks while the scroller has no height, so a hidden tab asks nothing; and the after-draw test does not ask while `olderError` is set, so only a scroll asks again.

   The reader's words stay where they are, by one rule for every draw: before a draw take the first row on screen and its top, and after the draw move `scrollTop` by that row's change, unless the reader is at the foot, where the column follows as today. This covers turns from this tab's read or another tab's, the muted line appearing and going, an append and a patched result in the same draw, and the loss of `.user:first-child` by a row that stops being first. The row is the anchor, kept by its key while the tab is hidden, so a tab shown again has the reader's words where they were; the anchor resets with `shown`. Browser scroll anchoring stays as it is, since the correction is absolute. While a read is out a muted line stands at the top of the list, and a failed read draws its sentence there. Nothing draws at the file's head.
7. **Comments, words and pages.** Every paragraph listed above says what is true after the change; the comment on `FIRST_READ_TURNS` stops saying older turns stay out of the view. Item 5's sentences of `turn-rows.ts` are rewritten too. `docs/HELP.md` gains one line in the Transcript list saying older turns load as you scroll up.

## Cases

Kept as tests, in the files named, since the turns a reader sees are what G40 guards and a turn lost or repeated at the seam misleads whoever reads the session:

- Route, a file of 500 turns: a first read answers the last 200 and a `head` above 0; `before=<head>` answers the 200 before them; `before=<that head>` answers the rest and `head` 0. The three answers joined equal the turns of a read from 0, in order, none lost or repeated.
- Route, the seam falls inside an assistant line holding a said turn and two calls: all three stand in one answer, and the older read answers none of them.
- Route, a call among the turns a first read drops, its result in the turns it keeps: that outcome is in the answer's `results`.
- Route, `before=0`: no turn, `head` 0. `before=abc` and `before=-1`: 400. `before` past the size: the same answer as a first read.
- Route, a file whose last 32 MiB before `before` hold no turn, a turn 40 MiB before it: that turn is answered.
- Route, an agent's transcript and a Codex rollout: `before` answers the turns before it, and an unknown agent is refused as G84 says.
- Route, a file of several pieces read with `before`: macrotasks counted as G148's test counts them.
- Store: a call whose result came in an earlier read, its turn arriving in a read of older turns: the turn reads that outcome.
- Store: `readOlder` twice while one is out asks once. At `head` 0 it asks nothing.
- Store: a tail read that lands while an older read is out, and the other order: every turn of both is held, in order, and `offset` and `head` are both the newer ones.
- Store: `forget` while an older read is out: its answer is not held.
- Store: a failed older read keeps the turns, sets `olderError`, and the next `readOlder` asks again.
- Store, reads chained to the file's head through `readOlder`: every turn held once, in order, `head` 0.
- Store, an older read out, `forget`, a new first read, then the old answer lands: it is not applied. The test settles one answer before the other lands.
- Route, the oldest kept line is 9 MiB, spanning `PIECE_BYTES`: `head` is its first byte and the next read holds none of its turns.
- Route, a first read that doubled past 32 MiB (the table row of the test file): `head` is the turn's line and the next `before=<head>` goes on.
- Route, the keys of a `from` answer and of a first answer are as item 2 says.
- Route, `from` and `before` together: `before` wins. `before=` empty: a first read.

## Decisions taken in this brief (each reversible, none silent)

1. Older reads ask by byte, `before`, rather than by turn index: the file has no index of turns, and a byte is what `offset` already is.
2. A read keeps 200 turns, as a first read does, and renders only those.
3. The trigger is one column height from the top, so turns are usually in before the reader reaches them.
4. A line's turns stay together, since the byte of a line is the only place a read can start.
5. No virtual list, as for the first read of the Transcript: turns are held only as far as a reader scrolls. `@tanstack/virtual-core` is in the dependencies and is not used here. A reader who scrolls a 4.4 GB session to its head holds all its turns until the tab stops showing it, and the report states the bytes and milliseconds of one `before` read on a sparse fixture, from a case's run.
6. Two sentences go into the catalog under `sessions.`: `turns_older` (Reading older turns) and `turns_older_failed` (Older turns could not be read: {0}).
7. `before` is a page-to-server query, documented on the route's head comment, as `from` is. No public page names it.
8. A file shrunk or rewritten between reads throws nothing and may repeat turns until the tab reopens; the report states it.
