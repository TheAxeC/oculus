## What to build

1. **The grid draws what is seen.** `CardGrid.svelte` takes the cards as data: the list, for each card its `each` key, its stable identity (its Reveal id, `identOf` and the like, the id the view's reveal names) and the words of its stand-in, a snippet that draws one card, the wanted id the view passes with its fold gating as today, and an optional trailing snippet always drawn after the list (the No funding card, Pillars' Orphans and Archival cards; `Collection.svelte:388` passes an empty list with its No funding card as that snippet). It draws, at first and whenever the list grows (a list arriving over the socket after the grid mounted), the cards in the pane's view and those within one view's height below it, and draws each further card when its stand-in comes within that distance of the view as the pane is scrolled. A drawn card stays drawn, kept by its stable identity, so a key that shifts when a card is added above (a title and index key) does not send drawn cards back to stand-ins. The card under a held card cursor is drawn, as `VirtualRows.svelte` keeps `nav.kept` rows. The watch is rooted at the pane's scroll box, as `FileRows.svelte` roots its own. The grid's layout rules stay as they are (columns, gap, one height per row, no reordering, a card never wider than its column).
2. **A card not yet drawn holds a stand-in.** Each card not yet drawn stands in its grid cell as an element `hidden="until-found"` holding the card's words (item 4: exactly the text the card's face draws) as text and no `data-card`, so the card cursor steps only onto drawn cards. Its height is that of a drawn card of its grid: a fixed first guess in `rem` named in the code, so it follows the text scale as `VirtualRows.svelte:12-14` does, replaced by the height of the first card of the grid measured above 0; a measurement of 0 (a pane in a hidden tab, before layout) is ignored. So a row neither jumps when its cards are drawn nor reorders, and the scroll bar spans the whole list. A find that matches a stand-in (the browser's, or the desktop app's find bar) fires `beforematch`; the grid then draws that card and the cards above it in its grid at once, as item 3 does, so the match lands in the drawn card, the page scrolls to it and it stays in view. A stand-in inside a folded group (`GroupHead.svelte`) is found the same way: the group's own `beforematch` unfolds it and the card's draws it. Cmd+F works exactly as it works today, with no change of behaviour (Axel): in the browser tab and in the desktop app's find bar, the same words match, the find bar shows the same count of matches, the matches come in the same order down the page, Enter and Shift+Enter step through them as today, and each match is shown highlighted in the drawn card, never in a stand-in left hidden.
3. **A revealed card is drawn first.** A card the palette or a link asks to reveal (the wanted id matching a card's stable identity) is drawn by the grid before it is scrolled to, so its `Reveal` scrolls to the drawn card and flashes it. The cards above it in its grid are drawn too, so the scroll position does not move under the reveal as they are drawn. `Reveal.svelte`'s code is unchanged; only its head comment changes, true for its users outside a grid (`Inbox.svelte:180`, `EntryList.svelte:115`) as for the grids.
4. **The words of a stand-in.** Each caller gives, for each card, exactly the text its card's face draws: its title and every other text field, tag and label the card component renders, for example Lit Watch's idea spark at `RadarCard.svelte:126`, its relevance tag at `:271-272` and its project titles, and nothing the face does not draw (ruling 712 (a)). So the browser's find, and the desktop app's find bar, find in a card not yet drawn exactly the words they find in it today, no more and no fewer: a word the view's search reads that the face does not draw (Lit Watch's project key, `radar.ts:103`) gives no match, as today. The text comes from the same fields the card component draws, read once, so the stand-in and the drawn card cannot hold different words. The report names, per caller, the texts given with the card component's lines they were read from (`file:line`).
5. **Every caller moves to the new grid.** All 15 grids of the 12 files take the new shape. Each keeps its `Reveal`, its card component, its keys and its attributes (`role="list"`, `data-linkedin-section`). A view's search, its filter bar, its group heads and their counts read the lists as today, so they still cover every card, drawn or not.
6. **Nothing else of a view changes.** A card drawn looks and acts as today (its menu, its keys, its drag where the view has one, its cursor). The list mode of the project board (`BoardList`) is not a card grid and is not changed.
7. **The words.** The head comments of `CardGrid.svelte`, `Reveal.svelte` and `GroupHead.svelte` (`:49-57`) say what each does now, with no history: a folded group's cards not yet drawn are found through their stand-ins. `docs/HELP.md` is not changed (ruling "Help redesigned"); a sentence of it the change makes false is named in the report with its line, for the orchestrator to bring to Axel.
8. **The time to show.** The time Lit Watch (on the scratch hub's 678 papers) and the research hub's Projects board take to show their cards, before and after, is measured by the orchestrator on the scratch server at landing; the builder runs no server. The mark is fixed: from the click that opens the view's tab to the frame in which the first view's cards are painted, read from a Chrome performance trace, three runs each, the mean given. The after time must be below the before time for both views; an after time not below it is a finding for the repair round. The report says what to open for each.

## Decisions taken in this brief (each reversible, none silent)

1. A drawn card stays drawn: the grid draws more as the pane scrolls and never takes a card out, so a card's state (an open menu, a field being typed in) is never lost by scrolling, and the stand-ins carry the browser's find.
2. A view with no search field for a card gives the card's title and the text fields its face draws, so the browser's find reaches what a reader sees on the card.
3. The distance ahead is one view's height, so a scroll at reading speed finds its cards drawn.
4. The watch is the browser's `IntersectionObserver`, as `FileRows.svelte` uses it.
5. A card outside the list is given as a trailing snippet the grid always draws, since it has no item, no search words and no reveal.
6. The stand-in holds exactly the text the card's face draws, as Axel ruled (ruling 712 (a)), so Cmd+F works exactly as today.

## Libraries checked

- `@tanstack/virtual-core` 3.17.11 (in `package.json:69`, MIT, TanStack; the ruled library for long lists of rows, Q82): it is headless and computes one contiguous range of items to draw, with flow spacers before and after it as `VirtualRows.svelte:4-7` uses it, and a `lanes` option for several columns. Ruling 705 keeps one element per card in the page for the browser's find; Decision 1 keeps a drawn card drawn, so the drawn set is scattered (cards found or revealed far down), which one contiguous range does not model; and `lanes` needs a fixed lane count, where the grid's columns come from CSS `auto-fill`. Not used; the watch is a few lines on `IntersectionObserver`.
- No other candidate: the capability is drawing a cell when it nears the view, which the browser gives.
