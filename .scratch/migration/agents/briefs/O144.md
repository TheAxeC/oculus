The hub's schemas have no type for when something happens, so one period is stored as text in up to four places that disagree (`date`, `duration`, a period inside `sub`, a When meta row), sorts as text, and is copied to the website's CV as written. ## What to build

1. **The reader of a period**, in `src/lib/data/helpers/dates.ts`, beside `ISO_DATE` (ruling 671 1 (a), no dependency), as EDTF level 0 and level 1's open end:
   - A date is `YYYY`, `YYYY-MM` or `YYYY-MM-DD`, the month 01 to 12, the day a day of that month in that year. A date-time is `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SS`, the hour 00 to 23, the minute and the second 00 to 59, optionally followed by `Z` or a zone `+HH:MM` or `-HH:MM`. A period is one date, one date-time, two dates joined by `/` with the second not before the first, or a date followed by `/..`, an open end; a date-time stands alone, never in a span. A value written as a whole number of four digits in YAML (`when: 2025`) is the year. Nothing else is a period: no spaces, no date-time without seconds, no other EDTF form (`?`, `~`, `%`, `X`, seasons, sets, an open start `../2025`).
   - It returns the start, the end (none for an open end, the start for one date) and each one's precision, or null.
   - It gives the order of two periods, newest first: the one whose start has the later first day comes first; on the same first day, the one whose end has the later last day comes first, an open end later than every end. "Not before" for a span compares the first day of the start with the last day of the end. So `2026` (first day 2026-01-01) comes after `2026-10-27/2026-10-30`, and `2025/..` before `2025` but after `2025-09/2026-09`.
   - The table test of G151 in `tests/data/helpers/period.test.ts`, its rows the cases C1 and C2 and its order test C3, under the rules file's test rule: a wrong period accepted or refused is a wrong value written to a hub or a finding missed.

## Decisions taken in this brief (each reversible, none silent)

1. A value written as a four-digit integer is a year, since the hub writes years that way (`year`, 50 manifests, all integers).
2. A `when` at month or year precision gives no radar day: the radar reads days, and a month's first day would be a day no one gave.
3. A `sub` that names another day than the entry's period keeps it ("thesis market Tue 24 Nov 2026, 12:30-13:30", "submit Apr 2027"): that day is not when the entry happens.
4. An award's CV `date` keeps the timestamp form the website parses, so `_data/data.yml`'s award lines read as before; the website stays as it is.
5. The open end prints "Current" in every CV, the employer CVs included, where the profiles' rows wrote "Present".
6. A lead's words beyond the day go to a new `note` field, and a lead may hold a deadline and `rolling` together, since postings say both ("2026-11-05 (rolling review, may close early)").
7. A student's period is the student's own `when` where it differs from the supervision entry's, else the entry's.
8. The CV's `duration` key stays: it is the website's name for the period text, read by its templates.
9. A time of day is EDTF level 0's date-time, to the second, and stands only alone, since level 0 intervals hold dates only; the hub's one time becomes `2026-10-27T11:00:00`.
10. Two periods order by the first day of their starts, then the last day of their ends, newest first, so a year sorts with its first day and the order is a total one.
11. Words of a collection entry's period that are not a period go to its `note`, since `about` is the CV text; words of a career row's period go to its `about`, which the CV printed with the period before.
