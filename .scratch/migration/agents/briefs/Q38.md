## What VS Code draws, the reference

- Editor tabs: 35px tall, no gap between tabs, a 1px border between them, the active tab on the editor background with a 1px top accent border (`tab.activeBorderTop`), inactive tabs on the tab strip background with muted text; the close control shown on the active tab and on hover, a dirty tab showing a filled dot in the close control's place until hovered; tab labels in the UI font at 13px with the file icon before the name; the strip overflows into a thin scrollbar.
- Editor group header: the tab strip and, at its right end, the group's actions (split, more `...`) as icon buttons.
- Sashes: 4px hit area, invisible until hovered or dragged, then a 1px or 2px accent line after a short delay.
- A second level of tabs does not exist in VS Code; oculus's page strip is the closest analogue to the window's title bar tabs of other editors and keeps its own place above the workbench.

## What to build

1. **The tab strip** in `ui/Tabs.svelte` takes VS Code's measures and marks: tab height, borders, active top border in the accent, inactive muted text, close control on the active tab and on hover, a dirty dot in the close control's place for a tab `unsaved` answers for, the view glyph before the label, badges kept after the label in the tab's height. Everything in theme tokens.
2. **The pane header** in `shell/PaneView.svelte`: the pane's actions as icon buttons at the strip's right end, the menu as a `...` button, the scroll controls of Q33 kept.
3. **The sashes** in `shell/SplitView.svelte`: a wider invisible hit area, the accent line shown on hover (after a short delay) and while dragging, the layout sizes unchanged (the visible thickness a token, the hit area outside the flow).
4. **The page strip** in `shell/PageStrip.svelte` uses the same tab look at its own height, told apart from the pane strips by its place and background.
