## The three judgment calls

1. **`model.ts` and `projects.test.ts` edited.** Sound, and I verified both reasons on the tree rather than taking the report's word.
2. **Publishing `files` before the git read.** Sound: a write landed whether or not `statusFor` throws or returns undefined, and the dropped-root guard still sits above the publish.
3. **The 50 ms `delivered()`.** This is a real-time wait standing in for one microtask turn, and it is the one thing I would change. The manual clock removes the timing dependence of the *throttle window*, not of the hub's delivery, so the report is right that the two are different hops; but the delivery hop is not a timing hop either. `recording().send` (`git.test.ts:209-211`) pushes synchronously and resolves immediately, and `channels.ts:113-124` holds a second message only while the first send is in flight, so what `delivered()` waits for is one turn of the microtask queue. The deterministic form is already in the file: `await until(() => client.got.length >= before + 2, WAIT)` (`git.test.ts:134-137`) returns the moment the message lands and spends the budget only on a failure. Two of the four calls wait for nothing at all: after `clock.fire()` the first publish is recorded synchronously, and `expect(client.got.length).toBe(before.got)` at `git.test.ts:359` is guaranteed by the manual clock, not by the wait. Fix: replace each `await delivered()` that precedes a positive assertion with `until()` on the expected count, drop the other two, and delete the helper. Cost today is 200 ms of pure wall clock across the two cases, and it is the shape of wait that goes red on a loaded machine.
