# Window guide

What the window is made of and how to work in it: the app and the browser tab, the rail, pages and the pane tree, tabs and panes, the palette and the status bar. The key table is in [HELP.md](../HELP.md#the-keys).

## The window and the app

oculus runs in a browser tab or in its own app. The app (run from the checkout or packaged as an `oculus.app`, as `README.md` says) is Electron: it starts the server itself, shows the page in a window of its own with a Dock icon, and ends the server when it quits, leaving the tmux sessions running. It adds what a browser tab cannot give the page: windows that remember where they were, a floating pane that becomes a window of its own (Move into New Window in the pane's and the tab's menus, Copy into New Window in the tab's, Restore into Main Window and Switch to Main Window in a popped-out window), the keys Cmd+W, Ctrl+Tab and Ctrl+Shift+Tab, Cmd+T (the palette on the symbols of the workspace), Cmd+N, Cmd+Shift+N, Cmd+Shift+W, Cmd+, (settings), Cmd+R (reload the window) and Cmd+F, a find bar, a folder sheet for Add Folder to Workspace, the PDF export of a deck from the Present view (printed by a hidden window, saved beside the deck), the audience window of the Present view's presenter screen (the slide alone, in full screen on another display when there is one and beside the window when there is not; Close Window with it focused closes it and ends the presentation), spelling suggestions in the text menu, and a dark window frame; a page in the app with no stored theme starts on vscode dark modern. The windows of the app share one arrangement as the browser tabs do. When the server ends on its own, a dialog quotes why and offers Restart, which starts the server again; the open windows keep their pages, their unsaved text and their running terminals, reconnect to the new server on their own and take its new write token, and a window on another port than the new server's goes to the new one, taking its arrangement, its settings and its unsaved text with it. A link to another site opens in the default browser. Started again, the app opens its windows where they were; a second start brings the running app forward. Everything else on this page is the same in both.

Down the left is the rail, and along the bottom is the status bar. Everything else is the pane tree: one tree per page, made of splits and panes, each pane a stack of tabs, each tab one view.

A control's tooltip opens when the pointer rests on it and when the keyboard brings the focus to it. A click puts it away, and it comes back once the pointer has left the control and rested on it again. Every scrolling box draws a thin scrollbar in the theme's colours, its thumb at 3:1 or more on its track, except the rail's list and the tab strips, which draw none and scroll by wheel, trackpad and their own controls.

## The rail

The rail draws one button per entry of the `rail:` list of `config/layout.yml`, each `{label, glyph, page}`, where `page` names an arrangement under `arrangements:`. As shipped there are twelve, top to bottom: Focus, Sessions, Projects, Incoming (the Inbox, Lit Watch, Position Leads, LinkedIn and Literature search views beside the Calendar), Explorer (Files, Repositories, Search and the TeX view, with an empty pane beside them), Academic Life, Pillars, References, Present, Apps (the apps found on this machine and in the roots, an empty pane until one is opened), Help (this manual and the HPC how-to beside Research approach, Agent approach, Data map, the Apps guide and the Memories wall) and System (Settings, Account setup, the Views catalogue, Pages, Status, Findings, Audit, History, the gallery and Processes). Every listed view sits on exactly one of these pages, in the pane its arrangement gives it, except Live HPC and Usage, which sit in the float docked at the right edge of the window and are on every page. The Views catalogue lists each page's views under the page's name, the Help page's under Help, reached by `h`. A button shows its page: made from its arrangement the first time and shown as it was left after that. A browser with no saved layout starts on the first of them, and draws no page until the configuration has named it. A button is an icon; hovering it shows its page's name and the digit that reaches it. It carries one badge, in the accent: the count of the first view of its page that counts anything, in the order of the page's tabs, the arrangement's views before the page is first made. Drag a button up or down the rail to move it; right-click it (or press Shift+F10) to hide it, to reset its page to its arrangement, or to open the rail list in Settings. Hiding and moving rewrite `rail:`, and History undoes either. Reset is offered once the button's page has been made, and asks first: the question names the page, and then each of its tabs holding text no save has taken is asked about as closing it would be, one at a time; a no to any leaves the page as it was. It rebuilds the page from its arrangement, under the button's name, closing every tab the page held. Hiding the page on screen leaves it on screen. Every view, and every hub document of the `docs` key, is listed in the Views catalogue on the System page, under its `nav_groups` group; the groups, their order and their letters are those two keys. A document tab saved at a path the `docs` list no longer holds follows the entry carrying its name, or closes into the tab already showing that entry; one that matches no entry names the path it asked for.

The rail is 48 px wide with 48 px square buttons and a 24 px icon at every text size, drawn in the page's ground colour with a 1 px border on its right edge. An icon is muted until it is hovered. The page on screen draws in the text colour with a 2 px accent bar on its left edge. The button's name and digit show as a tooltip centred beside it with an arrow pointing at it, and its badge is a 16 px accent pill over the icon's lower right corner.

## The Views catalogue

In the catalogue on the System page a click shows the page the reader was on before that page and opens the view there, where views land, which is the pane whose tab was activated last; after a load straight onto the System page it opens on the first rail page. A catalogue the reader has moved onto a page of their own opens the view on that page. A view already open is activated where it is rather than opened twice. A shift-click opens it in a new pane to the right, on that same page, and the next shift-click joins that pane as a tab while it is still open. Dragging an entry onto a pane of the page the catalogue is on opens it there. An entry's right-click menu, or Shift+F10, offers Open on this page, which opens it in another pane of that page (beside the catalogue when the catalogue's pane is the only one), and Open beside on this page, which opens it in a new pane beside the catalogue. A view that was a tab of the catalogue's own pane moves out of it, and that pane goes on showing the catalogue. A view already open in another pane of that page is activated where it is. A drop on the catalogue's own pane goes to another pane, so the catalogue is never covered.

## The foot of the rail

At the foot of the rail are Bookmarks, a menu rising from the star that lists the reader's own bookmarks from `~/.oculus/bookmarks.yml` by name, which the Files view adds and takes away, each opened as a tab of its own; Search, which opens the palette; and Update, which opens the update tray. Each is an icon with its name as the tooltip. The button of the page on screen draws its icon in the text colour with an accent bar on its left edge. The list above the foot scrolls on its own, and a fade at its top or bottom edge says more entries are past it. The appearance controls (Theme, Code theme, the Text row of smaller, reset and larger, CRT, Wallpaper where the theme has one, and Icons) are in the Settings view.

## The update tray and schedules

Update, at the foot of the rail, opens the update tray, which holds two sections under its heading. Schedules lists the entries of `~/.oculus/schedules.yml`, with Add schedule beside the heading. Tasks lists the four update tasks (Lit Watch, Scout, LinkedIn and Inbox), each with the date of its last run, a line naming the next run of the schedule that runs it (`Next: Mon 10-12 07:00 (Weekly radar)`, or `No schedule`) and a Schedule... control, and a Chat control that starts a portfolio-wide Claude session. A task runs the `/research-cockpit` skill as an ordinary Claude session in a terminal, so its permission prompts are answered by you in the terminal pane, which is where the tray puts you when the task starts; a press always starts it that way. A schedule runs an update task, a saved command or a prompt (the first message of a Claude session, started in the hub folder or in a root the file browser serves) on a cron pattern in the machine's local time. The dialog of Add schedule, and of Edit on a row, takes a name, what it runs, when it runs (Off, Every hour, Every day at a time, Weekly on chosen days at a time, or a Custom cron pattern) and Run unattended. It shows the next three runs of the pattern before it saves. Schedule... on a task, or in the right-click menu of a saved command, opens it with that task or command filled in. The file is YAML that may be edited by hand: the dialog, Pause and Resume change only the keys they change, and every other key, its order and every comment stay as written. It is a list of entries such as `- name: Weekly radar`, `runs: { task: litwatch }`, `when: "0 7 * * 1"`, with `paused: true` and `unattended: true` as optional keys. A saved command is `runs: { command: sync, folder: ~/workspace/research-hub }` and a prompt is `runs: { prompt: "Summarise what is blocked", folder: hub }`. An entry without `when` runs only from Run now. A row lists its latest five runs, and a run whose terminal is still listed opens that terminal; Pause and Resume, Run now and Delete act on the entry, and a Run now that starts a terminal shows it as a task press does. A run missed while oculus was not running starts once when it starts again. A due time that arrives while the schedule's earlier run is still working starts nothing and is listed as skipped; one that arrives while the earlier terminal is idle or has ended closes that terminal and starts a new run. A command chain that holds a command from a file or a found command needs its approval too, and a schedule that would run it is refused until it is approved from the command bar. Run unattended starts the schedule's runs with `--dangerously-skip-permissions` and applies to an update task or a prompt: with it on, a scheduled run lets an agent edit files and run commands with nobody asked, on a timer. A saved command always runs with it off, so a command entry whose `unattended` value oculus does not read is saved with it off. A schedules file or run record that does not read stops every schedule, is said in the tray, and is never written over. A save, Delete, Pause or Resume the tray cannot make is refused with its sentence, and nothing is written: "The schedules file could not be written, so nothing was saved.", "The run record does not read, so nothing starts.", "Another oculus is writing the run record; try again in a moment.", "The run record could not be written; check ~/.oculus/schedule.json and try again.", and, for an entry that shares a block with another through a YAML alias when the change cannot be written into the file, "The entry "X" cannot be changed from here; edit schedules.yml by hand.". A run whose due time came while oculus was stopping shows "failed: The server stopped before the run started.". A task or a Run unattended value in the file that oculus does not read opens in the dialog with none chosen, and Save asks for one. The keys `tasks.skip_permissions` and `chat.skip_permissions` are not read, and a file that carries one gets a finding on the Findings view that names Run unattended.

## Pages

A page is a whole working surface with a name: its own tree, its own tabs, its own arrangement. The rail's buttons switch to their pages. The Pages view on the System page lists every page as one row of the explorer's size: press a row to show its page, and hover or focus it for Rename and Close, Close asking first when the page holds more than one tab. The page on screen is the bold row with a check, a page of a rail button names its key after its name, and a page holding unsaved text carries the unsaved dot at the row's end. A page of a rail button also carries Reset to its arrangement, the same reset as the rail button's menu. Settings holds Reset all pages, which rebuilds every page of a rail button from its arrangement, removes the pages of buttons the rail no longer has and puts the floats of `config/layout.yml` back in place of the floats you have, leaving the pages you made yourself alone; it asks first, naming each page it rebuilds, each it removes and each float it replaces, then asks about each tab of those pages holding unsaved text as closing that tab would, one at a time, and a no to any of them ends the reset with nothing changed; it says so when there is nothing to reset. The digits 1 to 9 show the rail page of that place.

Switching tabs keeps each tab's view as it was left: a pane keeps the view of every tab it has shown until that tab closes.

A tab opens right after the active tab of its pane, after the pinned tabs when the active tab is pinned, and an unpinned tab never lands among the pinned ones. Closing the active tab shows the tab the pane showed before it that is still open, else the tab that took its place. Both are VS Code's defaults and have no setting.

## Where an opened tab lands

A view opened for something (a project, a person, a deposit, a manifest edit, a hub document, a file, a source file, a diff, a merge, a note, a customization file) never covers the tool views of the page it is opened on. It lands, in this order: in the pane that received the last such tab on this page, while that tab is still there; in the pane you are in, when that pane already holds such tabs; in an empty documents pane, then an empty terminals pane; in a pane holding such tabs and nothing else; and otherwise in a new pane split off to the right of the pane you are in, which the next one then joins. A pane holding a tool view takes one only when you are in it and it already holds opened tabs. A tool view that asks for a tab (the Search view's Replace Preview) is never landed in: its pane is taken out of every step above, and when no other pane takes the tab a new pane is split off to the right of it. A tab of that document already open anywhere on the page is activated where it is, and a maximised pane of tools is restored first so the landing pane shows. The rule does not move tool views. An empty pane says Files and documents you open on this page appear here; the Explorer page carries one beside Files, Repositories, Search and the TeX view. Closing or dragging out the last tab of a pane that held a view opened for something leaves the pane empty, a documents pane, and the last tab of a pane that was a Terminal tab leaves an empty terminals pane saying Terminals you start from this pane's menu appear here. A page keeps never more than one empty pane, a documents pane or a terminals pane, so a pane emptied while another empty pane is on the page closes, and Close pane removes a kept one. A pane whose last tab was a tool view closes, and the next view opened for something lands in an empty pane if there is one, else splits a new pane off to the right. Inside the pane it lands in, a new tab opens to the right of the active tab, after the pinned tabs when the active tab is pinned. Closing the shown tab shows the tab that was shown before it and is still open, and the order the tabs were shown in is kept with the layout. How a single click opens a preview tab, where a new tab opens and how many tabs a pane holds (no limit) are VS Code's defaults and have no setting.

## Inside a page

Inside a page:

- **Split.** Drag a tab onto the edge of a pane to split that pane and drop the tab into the new half. The pane menu splits without a drag.
- **Fold.** Every child of a split has one control that folds it to the split's edge and shows it again. A folded child is saved folded.
- **Maximise.** A pane can be drawn alone, the rest of the tree left where it is, and shown again from the same control.
- **Float.** A pane can float over every page: Float in the pane menu or in the right-click menu of the pane's strip floats the pane, and a tab dragged with Alt held (Option on a Mac) floats at the pointer wherever it is let go, an outline showing where. A floating pane has an orange border, stays on screen when the rail switches pages and while a pane of the page is maximised, and holds one pane of tabs, so it has no split, fold or maximise. Drag the empty part of its tab strip to move it and its edges or corners to resize it; let go within 24 pixels of the area's left, right or bottom edge to dock it there. A docked float is a strip along that edge with a handle on its inner edge that sets its size, by drag or by the arrow keys. Its menus offer Dock Left, Dock Right and Dock Bottom (not for the edge it is on), Float on a docked float, which floats it again, Return to Page, which puts its tabs back into the page on screen as a new pane split off the right of the largest pane, Move into New Window (in the app), which makes the float a window of its own where it is, and Close, which closes the float and asks as the pane close does. One float is docked to an edge, so docking a second there joins its tabs to the first. A float disappears when its last tab goes, is placed by fractions of the window, so it scales with the window and may lie over the rail and the status bar, and answers the keyboard as a pane does. Live HPC and Usage start as a float docked at the right edge, and Reset all pages puts the floats of `config/layout.yml` back.
- **Drag.** A tab moves inside its pane and to another pane, and a page's row dragged from the Pages view onto a pane's edge shows that page there. A row dragged and let go over itself shows nothing. A drop on a pane of a page shown inside another page lands in that page. Every drag has a menu equivalent that the keyboard reaches: the pane menu splits, floats, closes and maximises a pane and moves a tab to another page, and Shift-F10 opens the menu of the focused control.
- **Show a page.** A leaf of a page's tree can be another page, drawn where it sits with a header naming it. It is the same page, never a copy: a tab opened in it is open wherever that page shows. A page never contains itself and is drawn at most once on a screen.
- **The pane menu.** Close, maximise or restore, fold where the pane sits in a split, split right and split down, Float while the pane shows a tab, Move into New Window while the pane shows a tab (in the app), and two choices that open a list of pages to the side, Move tab to page... and Show a page here..., each offered only when there is a page to offer. ArrowRight or Enter on one of them opens its list, and ArrowLeft or Escape in that list goes back to the choice; Escape in the menu closes it. In any menu the arrow keys, Home and End move through the choices, and typing the start of a choice's name goes to it.
- **Right-click menus.** A right-click on the empty part of a pane's tab strip, or anywhere in an empty pane, offers Open File... (the palette, as Cmd-K opens it), New Text File (an untitled text opened in a source tab of that pane; its first Save asks for a folder, chosen among the workspace's served folders with the first selected, and for a name, which may hold folders that are made, and makes the file there; a name that leaves the folder is refused), Split Up, Split Down, Split Left, Split Right and Float while the pane shows a tab, Lock Group or Unlock Group (a locked pane takes no tab opened from elsewhere, though a tab can still be dragged onto it, and keeps its place when its last tab closes, so only Close pane removes it; a lock mark at the right end of its strip unlocks it when pressed; a float's pane is locked and unlocked the same way), Tab Bar (Multiple Tabs, Single Tab or Hidden) and Editor Actions Position (Tab Bar, named Title Bar while the tab bar is hidden, or Hidden; the choice in force carries a check mark in both submenus), which write `show_tabs` and `editor_actions` of `appearance.yml` and can be set in Settings, with Hidden also taking the `...` off the pane of a float, Close while the page has more than one pane (asking first as the pane menu's Close does), and New Terminal. A right-click on a tab opens the tab's menu, VS Code's in its groups and words: Close, Close Others, Close to the Right, Close Saved and Close All (each asks first when a tab it closes holds unsaved text or a running program; a pinned tab is closed by Close alone); for a tab showing a file, Copy Path, Copy Relative Path, Reveal in Finder, Show in Files, Open Timeline, Select for Compare and Compare with Selected; Keep Open (enabled on a preview tab) and Pin or Unpin; Split Right with a Split & Move submenu, and, in the app, Move into New Window and Copy into New Window (a Terminal tab offers no copy). A right-click on any other part of the page that has no menu of its own, a blank area included, shows no menu at all; the browser's own menu stays inside a text box, the code editor and the terminal. Every text box offers Undo, Redo, Cut and Copy over a selection, Paste and Select All, a read-only one only Copy and Select All; in the app, a right-click on a misspelt word in a text box shows the spelling suggestions at the top of that menu, a pick replaces the word, and no native menu is drawn; rendered Markdown and a document framed in the Document view offer Copy over a selection, Copy Image over an image and Open Image over an image of the hub, and nothing where there is neither. Shift-F10 or the context menu key opens the menu of the row or control that has the focus, and a line between items separates their groups. A right-click on a row of the Files view offers the file operations listed under the `files` view in the table below.
- **Tabs past the strip's width.** The strip scrolls rather than shrinking its tabs, and tab names are drawn whole. Its two controls move the left edge to the next tab's start, or to the strip's end past the last one; a tab being shown or pressed is brought in whole, and so is the shown tab when a narrower window crosses it or takes it out of sight. A wheel or trackpad scrolls the strip freely and leaves it where it stops, so a tab may show in part at either edge.
- **Wrapped tabs.** With `wrap_tabs: true` in `appearance.yml` (a toggle under Appearance in Settings, off by default) a strip of `show_tabs: multiple` puts the tabs it cannot fit on more rows instead of scrolling one row, and the pane's actions stay at the end of the last row; a tab dropped on a wrapped strip lands by the row under the pointer as well as by its place along the row. The key is ignored under `single` and `none`.
- **Pinned tabs.** Pin in a tab's right-click menu, Pin Editor in the palette (listed with Cmd-K Shift+Enter, for the active tab of the pane that last held the focus), or a drag into the pinned tabs, moves a tab to the start of its pane's strip, where it shows a pin in place of its close control (a press on the pin unpins; a pinned tab holding unsaved text shows its dot). Unpin, Unpin Editor or a drag out of the pinned tabs puts it back. A pinned tab keeps its pin when its pane floats, when it moves to another page and when its pane splits, and stays open through Close Others, Close to the Right, Close Saved, Close All and the Close All of Open Editors. Delete on a focused pinned tab closes nothing and shows the first tab that is not pinned.
- **The path over a file tab.** A tab showing a file (a source tab, a document, a PDF or another file view) has the file's path drawn under the strip as a breadcrumb in place of a file-name row: the root, each folder, the file. A press on the root or a folder shows the Files view at that folder, and a press on the file shows it in the Files view. In a source tab whose file has symbols (a Python, C or C++ file a language server serves, a Markdown or a LaTeX file), the path goes on with the symbols that hold the caret, outermost first, such as a class and then its method. A press on one moves the caret to it. With `show_tabs: single` the breadcrumb sits in the strip's row beside the tab, and with `none` it has a row of its own. When the bar is narrower than the path and the file's actions together, the path is cut from the left and the actions stay whole. The tab's tooltip holds the file's whole path with the home folder written `~`. A file tab draws its file's icon before its name, the one the Files tree draws for that file, in the strip and in Open Editors; a tab showing no file, a Terminal tab, a Files tab opened on a folder and a LaTeX tab among them, draws its view's icon, and a Markdown preview tab draws the preview's icon, as VS Code's does.
- **A file tab's actions.** The right end of the tab's breadcrumb bar carries the tab's own actions as icon buttons, each where it applies: Save (a source tab that may be written; disabled while nothing is unsaved) and Revert File (shown while the tab holds unsaved text), then Add Bookmark or Remove Bookmark (the star, filled when the file is bookmarked; a file under a served root), Open in Program (a file whose extension `open.program_ext` names an application for; none of `.md`, `.png` or `.html` has one as shipped, and `.pdf` names Preview), Build, View PDF and Build Log (a LaTeX source tab that is not a version at a commit and not a library file: Build shows the spinner while the root builds and offers Kill Build, View PDF opens the root's PDF beside the tab, Build Log opens the build's `.log` file as a source tab), Open Preview (a Markdown source tab not read at a commit; it opens the file's preview tab, kept, in the same pane), Open Preview to the Side (the same tab, which opens the file's preview tab in a new pane to the right, or shows the one already open), Submit Job (a `.slurm` file on disk under a served root; it saves unsaved text, then asks one question that names the project's code folder and the cluster folder it is copied to, how many files and how large, the rsync arguments and the `sbatch` command run over ssh, and on Submit sends the code and queues the job, then opens Live HPC; a script that never names `/local/$SLURM_JOB_ID` gets a warning first and its button reads Submit Anyway) and Toggle Blame (a source tab over a file of a repository, and the tab of a file at HEAD or in the index). A picture, a PDF, a download, an HTML document and a Doc carry the star and Open in Program where they apply; a PDF's bar carries the viewer's own controls (zoom, fit, find, the outline, thumbnails, page entry, scroll and spread modes, inverted colours and back) and a menu of its four ways to open (Open in New Tab, Open in New Window, Open in Browser and Open in Program), and a Markdown preview carries them with Open Source, which opens the file's source tab, kept, in the same pane. A refused bookmark write is said beside the star. The tab row's right end carries only Split Right and the `...` opener. The rendered half of a Markdown source tab and word wrap are palette actions, listed while the tab is on screen and each applies: File: Save and File: Revert File (a tab that may be written), Git: Toggle Git Blame Editor Decoration (a file in a repository, a version at HEAD or in the index included), Markdown: Toggle Rendered Half (a Markdown tab that may be written, off until turned on and kept per tab), Markdown: Open Preview to the Side, Slurm: Submit Job (a `.slurm` file on disk), and View: Toggle Word Wrap (Alt+Z). Cmd+S (Ctrl+S off macOS) saves the source tab on screen wherever the focus is and never opens the browser's own save dialog. The tab's right-click menu offers Add Bookmark or Remove Bookmark for a served file that is not read at a commit, Open in New Tab and Open in New Window for a PDF, Open in Browser for a picture, a PDF, a download or an HTML document, and Open in Program where `open.program_ext` names an application for the file (as shipped it names Preview for `.pdf`). The line saying what a save did stays in the source tab, under the editor.
- **How a strip is drawn.** Tabs stand edge to edge at one height, the shown tab on the pane's surface. The shown tab of the one pane a view opens into carries an accent line along its top and every other pane strip carries a plain one. A page drawn inside another page carries the pane accent only while the reader's active instance is inside that page, and a folded pane a view opens into carries the accent along its folded strip. A tab's close control shows on the shown tab and under the pointer. A tab's label is 13 px, its close control and pin 16 px, and a pane strip that does not hold the focus is drawn dimmer than the one that does. A tab holding text no save has taken shows a dot in the close control's place until the pointer is over it, and a screen reader hears its name followed by ", unsaved"; the Pages view marks a page holding such a tab as unsaved. The right end of a pane's strip holds, while the pane shows a tab, a split-right button, and the pane menu's ... opener; they and a strip's scroll controls are one bare icon each. At a large text size or in a narrow pane the shown tab keeps its title and its close control: a strip too narrow for its scroll arrows beside the tabs draws none, and its actions move to a line under the tabs, before a title is cut, so a pane about 100 px wide still shows the shown tab and its close control. A handle between panes, and between the Source view's editor and preview, shows nothing until the pointer rests on it, draws its line at once while dragged or when the keyboard reaches it, and is caught on the borders beside it as well as in its gap.

The layout is saved as it is changed, so the window comes back as it was left.

## Focus, tabs and unsaved text

Tab and Shift-Tab are the browser's: they move the focus between the controls on the page and reach no binding of this table, except in the source tab's editor, where Tab inserts one step of indentation; an Escape that closes nothing of the editor's, such as the find box or a suggestion, lets the next Tab or Shift-Tab move on. The rail is one stop of that order, and each strip of tabs is one stop with its pane menu the stop after it, so Tab steps past a whole strip in one press and the arrow keys move inside it; the stop stays on the tab the arrows left the focus on, and Delete closes the tab the focus is on. A tab holding text no save has taken is closed only after a confirmation, whether the close came from Delete or from the tab's own control. That text is a source tab's unsaved edit, a field of the manifest editor not yet saved, a value typed into an entry form that differs from the entry as the file holds it now, or a folder or workspace name typed into the Files view's root or workspace form and not yet added. The editor's fields, and in the Tasks, collection, Pillars, Domains, Venues and Data views the open add and edit forms with what was typed into them, and in the Files view the open bookmark form with what was typed into it and the root or workspace form with its line, are there again after the tab is switched away from and after a reload. Cancel, the view's Add or Edit control closing the form, a delete, a save and Revert let them go; a value typed into either form while its save is on its way is kept, in the form left open, and so is the text of an add form closed and opened again while its save was out; an add form closes once its entry is added only while nothing touched it since the save went, and one left open goes on writing the entry it added until its identity field is typed away from it, when it adds another; a bookmark form whose save renames its bookmark stays open over the bookmark under its new name; a save that would give an entry the name another entry of the same file already carries is refused, so no file ends up holding two entries a write cannot tell apart; and the Save control is not pressable while its own save is out. Closing a pane or a page, whether or not it asked first, lets go of what its own tabs held, as closing each tab would; a page shown inside the closed page is not closed with it and keeps what its tabs hold. In the Files view, + bookmark or a bookmark's edit control closing or moving the bookmark form lets what was typed go, and so do opening the root or workspace form over it and the bookmark it edits being taken away. A root, workspace or bookmark save answered after the reader opened another form or switched the tab away closes only the form it was sent from, and a root or workspace form closed and opened again while its add is out stays open with its line.

Ctrl and a digit still shows its rail page while a field, the palette, the update tray or a confirmation holds the keyboard; Ctrl and a bracket does nothing there, because a bracket is a character a field takes. The brackets cycle a pane's tabs; in the app Ctrl+Tab and Ctrl+Shift+Tab do too.

One tooltip is open at a time: a bubble opening closes the one that was open. A press on a control puts its tooltip away, a focus shows one only when it is the keyboard's, and the bubble returns once the pointer has left the control and rested on it again, so a click never leaves a bubble over what it opened. No tooltip opens during a drag, on the focus a closing menu gives back to its opener, or on the focus a dialog takes or gives back.

## The palette

Cmd-K opens the palette. With nothing typed it lists the views; a word typed searches the projects, the collection entries and the feed entries as well. A word matches fuzzily, its letters in order but not necessarily together, so `rpls` finds RAW-PLS, and the letters it matched are marked in each result's title; a result whose title matched comes before one that matched only elsewhere. The arrow keys, Home and End move through the results and Enter opens the one selected; the keyboard goes to the tab it opens, which becomes the page's active tab. Each result names what it is, View, Project, Collection, Feed, Editor or Command, before its title. Cmd-K and Ctrl-K open the palette from inside the source tab's editor too, and while a source tab is on screen the palette also lists the editor's own Cmd-K commands, such as Add Line Comment or Fold All, each named Editor with its old keys beside it; choosing one runs it in the tab. Pin Editor and Unpin Editor are among them, with Cmd-K Shift+Enter beside them, and act on the active tab of the pane that last held the focus. While a Present tab is on screen the palette also lists its commands, Edit Slide, New Slide, Duplicate Slide and Delete Slide for the slide shown, and Export to PDF, each named Command. Choosing one runs it in that tab. A result whose title is longer than the row is cut with an ellipsis on one line, and its tooltip holds the whole title. A colon starts a command: `:` and a line number moves the source tab on screen to that line, and `:language` lists Auto Detect (the language the file name gives), Configure File Association for the file's ending (a second list whose choice is written into the `languages:` map of `sources.yml`, undoable in History), Configure the language's settings (opens Settings on `editor.languages`) and then every language the highlighter knows, each with its name in brackets and the one in force marked Configured Language, narrowed fuzzily by what follows with the matched letters marked, to repaint the tab. A `#` starts a search of the workspace's symbols (Cmd+T in the app opens the palette with it typed): the symbols the language server of the source tab on screen, by its language and root, lists for the word after the `#`, each with its kind, its container and its file; Enter opens the file with the symbol's name selected, and a tab no server serves, or a server that is not running, says so in the list. While a Python tab is on screen, `:interpreter` lists the interpreters of its repository and takes a typed path, to choose the one pyright runs with; while a C or C++ tab is on screen, `:compile-database` lists the compile databases and the `.oculus/cpp.yml` configurations of its repository, to choose the one clangd runs with.

## The key sheet

The sheet itself opens with `?` and closes with `?` or Escape. The letters before a digit are the `direct_chord` of `config/layout.yml` and the `chord` of each group in `nav_groups`. A group's documents follow its views in its chord, in the catalogue's order, so the digit after the group's last view opens its first document, up to the ninth entry, since a chord is a letter and one digit; the directly drawn groups' documents follow the direct views under the direct chord the same way. Every view now sits in a group with a letter of its own, so the direct chord reaches nothing and the key table has no row for it; a group that names no `chord` gives it entries again.

## The status bar and notifications

The status bar runs along the bottom under the rail. On the left: the machine oculus runs on (opens Status), the branch of the repository holding the active tab's file (a list of its branches to switch to, after a confirmation), how far it is behind and ahead of its upstream (after a confirmation, pushes when the branch is only ahead and otherwise pulls and then pushes; a branch with no upstream shows Publish Branch in its place), the findings the Findings view lists (opens it), the debug item once a debug session has run since the server started (the name of the last configuration with a bug icon; a press lists the repository's configurations, and picking one starts it), the Live HPC poll, the usage refreshers and the server connection. On the right, for a source tab over a file of a repository: the caret line's blame, whether or not Git: Toggle Git Blame Editor Decoration is on (opens its commit in the Repositories graph; a tab over a file at HEAD or in the index shows its blame in the gutter only, since VS Code shows the status bar's blame for a working file alone), the line and column (opens the palette on `:`), the indentation (switch spaces or tabs and width, or convert the text), UTF-8, the line ending (convert it). In a file a language server serves, the bar then shows the server's state by its name (starting, indexing with its count, ready, not found, failed or root not served; a press lists Restart Language Server and Show Language Server Output). In a Python file it shows the interpreter in force as its version and environment, such as `3.13.4 (.venv)` (a repository's own interpreter says not approved until it is; a press lists the interpreters), and in a C or C++ file the compile database in force (a press lists the databases). Then the language (a searchable list that starts with Auto Detect, Configure File Association and Configure the language's settings, then the languages). Then the live sessions, the unsaved tabs (shows the first; not pressable at 0) and the bell. The LaTeX item is last of the left group: it shows while the active tab is a LaTeX source or the PDF of a build, or while a build runs, as a spinner and Build with the step while the root builds, a check after a successful build, a cross after a failed or stopped one and a hammer before any build ran in this page; its tooltip names the root, the recipe, when the build ended and the words counted, and a press shows the TeX view. While a debug session runs or is paused the whole bar is painted in the theme's orange. A right-click on the bar hides or shows any item. The bell counts unread notifications and opens the notification centre, with Clear, Clear All and Do Not Disturb, which stays on after a reload; a new notification shows as a toast for eight seconds. Notifications come from a refused rail change, a session waiting on a permission prompt or waiting for input after its turn, an update task that ran, a Live HPC job that ended, and a write that was refused or got no answer.
